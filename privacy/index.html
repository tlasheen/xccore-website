XC Core: Privacy Policy and Terms of Service

Effective date: 28 November 2026

Privacy Policy

In short

  • XC Core has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. We don't sell or share data about you.
  • Your flights, your location and your settings stay on your phone. We have no server that receives them. When you claim a flight on XContest, its IGC file and your XContest login go to XContest, over an encrypted connection (HTTPS); see Claiming a flight on XContest below.
  • The app downloads maps and terrain from the providers listed below. Like any website, they see your internet address (IP address) and which map area is asked for. Terrain is also downloaded by itself where you are, so the terrain server learns roughly where you are (you can switch this off).
  • The app is free, with no purchases. Every mode, XC included, is free; XC mode is free for a limited time.

Who is responsible

XC Core is made by Tarek Lasheen, Rehab City, Egypt ("we", "us"). Contact: tlasheen@gmail.com.

What the app uses on your phone, and why

All of this is processed on your phone only and never sent to us.

  • Precise location (GPS): your position, speed and height for the vario, the map, the flight statistics, the thermal analysis and the voice coach, takeoff and landing detection, and the low-height warning. The app uses your location while its flight screen is open, also with the screen off or locked so the vario and the recording go on in your pocket: on Android a notification shows it ("XC Core is running" or "Recording flight"), on iPhone the location sign does. It does not use your location once the app is closed, and asks for no permission to use it in the background.
  • Motion and pressure sensors (barometer, accelerometer, gyroscope, at a high rate): the vario's climb and sink. On iPhone, iOS asks for "Motion & Fitness" before the barometer can be read.
  • Bluetooth (only if you use an external vario, Settings → General → Sensors → External vario): the app searches for Bluetooth instruments nearby and shows their names, connects to the one you choose, and reads what it sends: pressure, climb, temperature, battery and, if it has one, its GPS position. These are used like the phone's own sensors. On Android 12 and later the app asks for the "Nearby devices" permission when you search, on iPhone for "Bluetooth"; it never uses the search to find where you are. On iPhone only Bluetooth Low Energy instruments can be used.
  • A Huawei watch (only if you switch it on, Settings → General → Huawei watch, in a version of XC Core that has the watch link): the app sends the flight's numbers to XC Core's app on your watch once a second (climb, altitude, height, speed, flight time) and its warnings, through the Huawei Health app and Bluetooth. A track you record on the watch alone (its GPS positions and altitudes) comes back the same way and is saved in your flight log.
  • Sound and headsets: the app plays the vario's beeps, warnings and spoken calls. It checks whether a headset is connected (its type and name) to choose the volume for a headset or the phone's speaker, and it reads the headset's button while the app is in use. The app does not use the microphone.
  • Speech: the calls are spoken by your phone's own text-to-speech engine (on iPhone, Apple's voices on the phone). The app prefers a voice stored on the phone. If the engine uses an online voice instead, the text of each call (for example "Tighten.") is sent to the engine's provider under that provider's privacy policy.
  • Battery and temperature: for the "battery low" and "phone too hot" warnings, and (Android) whether the phone's battery optimisation applies to XC Core, so the app can tell you if the phone may close it with the screen off.
  • Notifications (Android): a silent notification while XC Core runs with the screen off and while a flight is recorded, on the lock screen too.
  • Brightness (Android and iPhone), Do Not Disturb and volume (Android only; iOS lets no app change them, so on iPhone the app reminds you to switch on a Focus and turn the volume up): while a flight is recorded, XC Core can set the screen to full brightness, switch on Do Not Disturb (only if you allow it in the phone's Do Not Disturb access settings) and turn the media volume up, and puts back what it changed afterwards. It reads whether Do Not Disturb is on and its settings for media, only to decide this; nothing leaves the phone.
  • Files you choose: a flight file (IGC) you open for a replay, and a screen layout file you import, are read on the phone. A layout file you share holds only where the readings sit on the screen; it goes only where you send it.
  • Your photos (only when you tap "Save to Photos" on a route badge): the app adds the badge's picture to your phone's gallery (on Android in Pictures › XC Core; on iPhone it asks you first). It never reads or changes your other photos.

What is stored on your phone

  • The app's own log (since version 1.0.11): what happened while the app ran, for problem reports: sensor rates, errors and crashes, the coach's calls, the watch link's and the external vario's states, when recordings start and end. Bluetooth and e-mail addresses are removed and positions cut to about 100 m before a line is written. It is kept in the app's private files, at most about 1 MB (the oldest lines are dropped), and leaves the phone only in a problem report you send.
  • Your settings, including your screen layouts, your glider's polar and a landing point if you set one (a place on the map you choose, used for the glide to it).
  • What you enter under Pilot & equipment, all of it optional: your name, country and licence number, your wing, paramotor and engine, the engine's hours, maintenance notes and dates, and your reserve's last repack. Your name, your wing's make, model, size and class are written into each new flight file (IGC), as flight files usually carry them, so they leave your phone only inside a flight file you choose to share.
  • Tracks recorded on your Huawei watch, if you use it: saved in the flight log like your other flights, and deleted from the watch once saved.
  • The external vario you chose, if any: its name and the phone's identifier for it, so the app connects to it again. While it is in use, its make and model (only for instruments the app knows, otherwise "Bluetooth vario") are written into your flight files (IGC), never its name or identifier.
  • A waypoint route, if you enter one: the positions you type or paste, the names you give them, and the routes you save by name. With each flight that flew a route, which waypoints were reached or skipped and when, and the track drawn on its badge. The flight file (IGC) gets a comment line for each waypoint reached or skipped (its number and the time, not its name).
  • Map areas and terrain you download for use without a mobile signal.
  • Your flight recordings (IGC files) and flight log: every flight recorded, with the names you give takeoff sites.
  • A raw sensor log, only if you switch it on (Settings → General → Sensors), and a replay flag log, only if you use the flag button in a replay.
  • The last version file of the update check, and when it was checked.

They stay on your phone until you delete them or uninstall the app.

Android backup: if backup is switched on for your phone and it has Google's backup (phones with Google services), Android copies XC Core's settings and your flight data (your flight recordings and flight log) to the backup in your own Google account, so they come back when you move to a new phone. Downloaded maps and terrain and the raw sensor log are left out; download them again on the new phone. Google keeps the backup under its own privacy policy; we have no access to it. You can switch backup off in your phone's settings. A phone without Google services (for example a Huawei phone with XC Core from AppGallery) has no such backup for XC Core: share your flights to keep a copy.

iPhone backup: if iCloud Backup is on, or you back your iPhone up to a computer, iOS includes XC Core's settings and flight data in that backup, as for any app, under Apple's privacy policy; we have no access to it. A remembered XContest login is kept in the iPhone's Keychain, only on this device.

What leaves your phone

  • Map tiles, from the map style you choose: OpenTopoMap or OpenStreetMap (OpenStreetMap Foundation). Each request contains your IP address, the app's name and the map tile asked for, which shows roughly which area you are looking at, usually near where you are.
  • Terrain heights, from the Terrain Tiles open dataset on Amazon Web Services: when you download terrain for an area, and by itself when the flight screen gets a GPS position where your phone has no terrain yet (the area of one degree of latitude and longitude, about 110 km across, that you are in; switch this off in Settings → General → Terrain → Download terrain automatically). Each request contains your IP address and the area downloaded, so the terrain server learns roughly where you are: which area of one degree, not your position.
  • Update check: when the app starts or comes back to the screen, on the ground only and at most every 6 hours, it downloads a small version file and its signature from GitHub Pages. GitHub sees your IP address, as with any web request; the app sends nothing. To update, the app opens the store it came from: Google Play (its in-app update) in the Google Play version, XC Core's page in AppGallery in the AppGallery version, the App Store on iPhone (when you tap Update, the app asks Apple's public lookup, itunes.apple.com, for XC Core's App Store page; Apple sees your IP address and the app's identifier).
  • Sharing a flight, only when you choose to: through your phone's share menu to the app you pick (a flight file: IGC, or KML for Google Earth). An IGC file holds the track and, if you entered them under Pilot & equipment, your name and your wing. A route badge you share or save is a picture with the route's name, the waypoints' names and times and the distance, and, unless you switch on "Hide positions and map", the waypoints' positions and a drawing of your track (no map from a map provider).
  • Reporting a problem with a flight, only when you choose to (Settings → About, or a flight's details in the Flight log): the app makes one zip file with the flight's IGC file (see above: the track, and your name and wing if you entered them), the app's log from 10 minutes before takeoff to 10 minutes after landing, your note, the app's version, the phone's (or watch's) model and system version and the main settings (no names, no routes, no landing point, no device addresses). It shows you every file in it first, then opens your e-mail app with the zip attached, addressed to XC Core's support address (tlasheen@gmail.com). Nothing is sent until you send that e-mail yourself; we use the report only to find and fix the problem, and delete it when it is no longer needed.
  • Claiming a flight on XContest, only when you tap Claim on XContest:
    • Directly from the app (where your version offers it): the app sends to XContest (www.xcontest.org, over HTTPS) the flight's IGC file (your track with its positions, times and altitudes, and your name and wing if you entered them under Pilot & equipment); what you choose on the claim screen (public or not, the class, the glider and its category, the engine, and XContest's own questions); your XContest username and password, over an encrypted connection (HTTPS) and to XContest only, as XContest requires. XC Core's own key for XContest's service goes with each request, so XContest knows which app is asking.
    • "Remember my XContest login" (off unless you switch it on) keeps your username and password encrypted on the phone with a key in the Android Keystore (on iPhone in the Keychain, this device only), never in a backup, and sent nowhere but to XContest. "Forget my XContest login" (Settings → Pilot & equipment) deletes it. Without it, the login is kept in memory only while the app runs.
    • A claim made without a network waits on the phone (the flight's id and your choices) and is sent when the phone is online again.
    • Once there, the flight and your login are XContest's, under XContest's privacy policy; to change or delete a claimed flight, use XContest's website.
    • Or by sharing the file through the share menu and claiming it on XContest's own website, which the app opens for you; then the app itself sends nothing to XContest.
  • To your Huawei watch, only if you switch it on: the flight's numbers and warnings go from your phone to your watch through the Huawei Health app over Bluetooth, not over the internet. Huawei's Wear Engine (in Huawei Health) checks with Huawei's servers that XC Core is allowed to reach your watch, and uses your watch's name, model, identifiers and status for the connection; Huawei says it doesn't store them. Huawei Health and its privacy policy are Huawei's; XC Core itself sends nothing to Huawei.

Nothing else: no analytics, no crash reports, no advertising identifiers.

Your data protection rights

Where a data protection law applies to you (for example the GDPR in the European Union, or Egypt's Personal Data Protection Law), we process only what is needed to give you the features you use, and for map, terrain and update requests our legitimate interest in showing you a map and keeping the app working. As we hold no data about you on any server of ours, there is normally nothing for us to give you, correct or delete, but you may contact us with any request, and you may complain to your data protection authority.

Your choices

  • Settings → General → Permissions lists every permission XC Core uses, why, and whether it is allowed, with a button to the phone's settings.
  • You can take back the location permission in your phone's settings at any time. The flight screen needs it: without it the vario does not start.
  • To stop XC Core (and its use of your location) with the screen off, close it: swipe it away from the recent apps (on iPhone, swipe up and hold, then swipe XC Core away).
  • You can switch the app's notifications off in your phone's settings; the app keeps working.
  • You can switch off full brightness, Do Not Disturb and the volume in flight (Settings → General → Display), and take back the Do Not Disturb access in your phone's settings.
  • You can forget the external vario (Settings → General → Sensors → External vario) and take back the Bluetooth ("Nearby devices") permission in your phone's settings; the phone's own sensors are used.
  • You can switch off "Send to the watch" (Settings → General → Huawei watch) and take back XC Core's permission in the Huawei Health app. (The watch links are on Android only.)
  • On iPhone, Settings → XC Core in the Settings app is where each permission (Location, Motion & Fitness, Bluetooth, Photos) is changed.
  • You can switch off the automatic terrain download (Settings → General → Terrain), and then download terrain yourself for the areas you choose.
  • Settings → General → Import & export settings writes all your settings to a file only when you tap Export, and hands it to the app you choose in the share menu; nothing is sent by XC Core itself. The file can hold your name and wing, your remembered landing point and route, and your external vario's Bluetooth address, so keep it as private as your settings.
  • You can delete map areas, terrain and logs in the app, or everything by uninstalling it.

Children

XC Core is a tool for paragliding pilots. It is not directed at children under 16, and we do not knowingly collect data from them.

Changes

XC Core is a flight aid. It is not a certified flight instrument, and it is not approved for navigation, for keeping clear of airspace, terrain, obstacles or other aircraft, or for any other safety-critical purpose.
