Features

Everything here is in the Android beta today. The app and its voice are in English.

Four flight modes

XC Core asks how you want to fly each time you open it, and remembers your last choice.

  • Speed: large height above ground, ground speed and heading, with no map. A turbulence bar, and a low-height alarm (a tone, words and a red flashing edge) at a height you set.
  • Soaring: a live lift line ahead of you, green for lift and red for sink, built from your earlier passes along the ridge. It's kept for each site and fades with age. A wind arrow, and a Thermal button for the 3D view.
  • XC: the map while gliding, the 3D thermal view when you circle, the voice coach, and a thermal compass pointing to the best or nearest thermal you can reach, with its distance and your arrival height.
  • PPG: for paramotor pilots (see below).

The flight screen is yours to arrange: on the ground, move, resize, add or remove widgets for each mode, and share a layout as a file.

Voice thermal coach

As soon as you start circling, XC Core maps the lift around your circle and tells you what to do, at the moment to act: "Open up", "Tighten", "Straighten now", or "Centred, hold it". It never gives the opposite correction within the same circle.

On a glide it notices lift ("Thermal here, turn right") and guides you back to the best thermal of the flight that you can still reach, corrected for wind drift: "Thermal, 800 metres, at 2 o'clock."

  • Choose how much it says (Quiet, Normal or Coach) and how often (Low, Medium or High).
  • Separate volumes for a headset and the speaker, switched by itself. If the headset drops out, it says so and carries on through the speaker.
  • Uses your phone's own speech engine, and checks that an offline English voice is installed.

3D thermal view

A chase view from behind your wing shows the circles you've flown, coloured by climb, the estimated core at your height and which way the thermal drifts. A wind estimate from your circling is shown with its uncertainty, and hidden when it isn't reliable.

In XC mode it opens by itself when you circle; in Soaring mode, tap Thermal.

Waypoint routes and the route badge

  • Type or paste waypoints (degrees and minutes, decimal degrees or a Google Maps link), or hold the map to drop them. Drag, rename, reorder, undo, and save routes by name.
  • A waypoint compass beside the thermal compass, in any mode.
  • The voice counts you in from 100 metres: "Waypoint 1 reached. Next: Goal, at 2 o'clock."
  • Reached waypoints go into the IGC file.
  • After landing, a route badge with your times, square or story-sized, to share or save. "Hide positions and map" keeps your spots private.

Offline maps and terrain

Topographic maps (OpenTopoMap) and OpenStreetMap. Download a region before you go, with its size shown, and the map works with no signal.

Terrain heights (SRTM) download by themselves for the area you're in, so height above ground keeps working offline; where there's no terrain, the app shows height above takeoff and says so. The thermal compass uses terrain to judge which thermals you can reach.

IGC logging and XContest

  • Takeoff and landing are detected by themselves, or tap Start flight. A drive or a cable car doesn't start a flight, and ridge soaring doesn't end one.
  • Every flight is saved as a detailed IGC file, written the way a dedicated logger writes it: the full header, GPS and pressure altitude, one fix a second.
  • A flight log with totals, the track coloured by climb, an altitude graph and replay. Export KML for Google Earth.
  • Claim a flight on XContest straight from the app, or share the IGC file.
  • Replay any IGC file through the whole app, with sound, coach and 3D view.

XC Core's IGC files aren't signed yet (no G record), so XContest accepts them but scores them at 0 points.

PPG mode

Climb, ground speed, altitude, flight time and the clock in large numbers, and nothing else: no map, no 3D view, no coach. The climb rate is held steady through engine vibration, and a running engine or taxiing doesn't start a flight. Large buttons.Your paramotor and engine hours go into your pilot details.

Made for the air, screen off

  • The vario, voice, GPS and recording keep running with the screen off or the phone locked in your pocket.
  • Headset button: one press reads your status, two repeat the last call.
  • Spoken warnings, each with its own tone: low height, strong sink, GPS lost, barometer failure, low battery, phone too hot, headset lost.
  • Brightness and Do Not Disturb set for the flight, and put back after landing.
  • The vario stays quiet on the ground until your flight starts; set exactly where the climb beep and sink tone begin.

And more

  • A real variometer

    Barometer, GPS and accelerometer combined, so the vario reacts to lift at once. Calibrated to sea level by itself; set QNH by hand if you prefer. Works on GPS alone on phones without a barometer.

  • External Bluetooth varios

    Use the sensors of a Bluetooth vario such as the Stodeus UltraBip. See watches and sensors.

  • Sensors and permissions

    A table of your phone's sensors and how good they are, a permissions page, and import or export of all your settings.

  • Free

    No ads and no purchases. XC mode is free for a limited time.

XC Core is a flight aid, not a certified instrument. Its readings and suggestions can be wrong: keep watch outside, fly within your skills and make your own decisions.
